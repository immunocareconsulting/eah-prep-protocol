HIV Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP) Prescribing Protocol
for Long-Term Care Facilities
SBAR Structured Recommendation & Provider/Facility Instructions — eClinicalWorks → Xcel Rx → PointClickCare Workflow
Document Type: Clinical Protocol / SBAR
Program: HIV Prevention & PrEP Services
EHR Platforms: eCW · Xcel Rx · PCC
Effective: 2025  |  Review: Annually
Distribution: Contracted Providers, Facility POC
⚕ Clinical Classification: HIV Prevention / Infectious Disease 🏥 Setting: Long-Term Care (LTC) / Skilled Nursing Facility 💊 Medication Class: Antiretroviral – PrEP 🔒 Compliance Tags: CMS F755 · F758 · IDPH Title 77

Purpose of This Document: This SBAR provides a standardized, step-by-step protocol for Equity Alliance Health (EAH)-contracted HIV providers and LTC facility staff to ensure HIV Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP) medications are prescribed, dispensed, received, ordered in the clinical record, and administered to residents without delay — and in full compliance with applicable CMS, IDPH, and facility standards.

Scope: Applicable to all EAH-contracted prescribers and LTC facility points of contact (POC) managing residents for whom PrEP has been clinically indicated.

S
Situation
Problem identification & clinical context
⚠️
Identified Problem: LTC facilities are reporting significant delays in receiving PrEP medications from the pharmacy after orders have been placed in PointClickCare (PCC), resulting in residents missing scheduled doses. This medication administration gap creates non-compliance exposure during regulatory surveys and places residents at increased HIV acquisition risk.
Clinical Context

HIV PrEP (e.g., Truvada®, Descovy®, Apretude®) requires uninterrupted daily or scheduled administration to maintain protective efficacy. Any gap in administration renders the resident unprotected and out of clinical protocol.

Survey Risk

CMS surveyors may cite the facility under F755 (Pharmacy Services) and F758 (Unnecessary Medications), as well as medication administration tags, when active orders cannot be reconciled with documented administration records (MAR).

B
Background
Clinical, regulatory & operational context
1
EAH Contracted Provider Role

Equity Alliance Health of Illinois contracts with licensed prescribing providers to deliver HIV prevention services, including PrEP evaluation and prescribing, for LTC facility residents. Prescribing occurs within eClinicalWorks (eCW) and is transmitted electronically to Xcel Rx pharmacy.

2
Dual EHR Environment

LTC facilities utilize PointClickCare (PCC) as their primary EHR for resident medication management and MAR documentation. PrEP prescriptions originate in eCW/Xcel Rx, but must also be entered as an active medication order in PCC with the correct start date to ensure lawful administration and surveyor compliance.

3
Root Cause of Delays
  • PCC order is entered before medication physically arrives at the facility
  • Facility does not notify the prescribing provider when medication arrives
  • PCC order start date does not match actual medication receipt date
  • Communication gaps between pharmacy, facility, and prescriber
4
Applicable PrEP Medications (Xcel Rx)
  • Emtricitabine/Tenofovir DF (Truvada® or generic): 200/300 mg once daily orally
  • Emtricitabine/Tenofovir AF (Descovy®): 200/25 mg once daily orally
  • Cabotegravir (Apretude®): Injectable – every 2 months after initiation series
  • Prescribe per current CDC PrEP guidelines, patient renal function, & hepatitis B status
A
Assessment
Step-by-step prescribing & workflow protocol
Phase 1 — Prescribing in eClinicalWorks (eCW) & Transmit to Xcel Rx
1
Open the Resident Encounter in eCW

Log into eClinicalWorks. Navigate to the resident's chart. Open or create an encounter for HIV Prevention / PrEP management. Document the clinical indication, HIV-negative status confirmation, STI screen, renal function (eGFR/CrCl), and hepatitis B serology in the encounter note.

2
Enter the PrEP Prescription in eCW
  • Navigate to: Orders → Medications → New Rx
  • Select the appropriate PrEP agent per clinical indication and guidelines
  • Enter dose, route, frequency, quantity, and days supply
  • Add clinical notes: "HIV PrEP per EAH protocol – LTC Facility Resident"
  • Attach the prescribing provider NPI, DEA (if applicable), and EAH practice address
3
Transmit via eRx to Xcel Rx Pharmacy
  • Select Xcel Rx as the designated pharmacy from the eCW pharmacy directory
  • Confirm NCPDP/NPI pharmacy ID is correct before transmitting
  • Send prescription electronically via eCW eRx module
  • Document transmission confirmation number in the encounter note
4
Alert the Facility Point of Contact (POC) — Immediately After Transmission

The prescribing provider (or EAH care coordinator) must notify the designated facility POC that a PrEP prescription has been sent to Xcel Rx. Notification must include:

  • Resident name and date of birth
  • Medication name, dose, and frequency
  • eCW transmission date and confirmation reference
  • Instruction: "Do NOT enter the PrEP order in PCC until the medication is physically onsite and confirmed by pharmacy delivery. Notify the prescribing provider immediately upon receipt."
Phase 2 — Facility Receipt, Notification & PCC Order Entry
5
Facility POC: Confirm Medication Arrival from Xcel Rx

Upon delivery from Xcel Rx, the facility nursing or pharmacy staff must:

  • Verify medication name, resident name, dose, and quantity match the prescription
  • Document receipt date and time in the facility medication receipt log
  • Secure medication in the resident's medication administration system
6
🔔 Facility POC: Notify the Prescribing Provider — Medication is Onsite

The facility POC must contact the prescribing provider on the same day medication is received and confirmed. Notification must include:

  • Resident name, medication received, and date/time of receipt
  • Confirmation that medication matches the prescription transmitted via eCW
  • Request for the prescribing provider to now enter the order in PCC
✅
Critical Control Point: The PCC medication order must NOT be entered prior to physical receipt of the medication. The PCC start date must equal the date the medication arrives at the facility.
7
Prescribing Provider: Enter Medication Order in PointClickCare (PCC)

Upon notification from facility POC that medication is onsite:

  • Log into PointClickCare and navigate to the resident's chart
  • Go to: Medications → Add Order
  • Enter: medication name, dose, route, frequency, prescriber information
  • Start Date = Date of medication receipt at facility (as confirmed by POC)
  • Add clinical notes: "HIV PrEP — EAH Contracted Provider — Start per date of facility receipt per EAH Protocol"
  • Verify order against eCW/Xcel Rx prescription for accuracy
  • Sign and activate the order in PCC
ℹ️
The PCC order start date must align with the actual date the medication was received at the facility — not the eCW prescription date, not the transmission date — to ensure the MAR accurately reflects when administration is to commence.
Phase 3 — Facility POC: Order Confirmation & Administration
8
Facility POC: Confirm the PCC Order
  • Review the active PrEP order in PCC against the Xcel Rx dispensed label
  • Confirm: medication name, dose, route, frequency, and start date match
  • Confirm: the order is active and visible on the resident's MAR
  • Document confirmation in PCC under the order notes
  • Escalate any discrepancies to the prescribing provider immediately
9
Facility POC: Ensure Medication Administration per Applicable Guidelines

The facility POC is responsible for ensuring administration is carried out by licensed nursing staff in compliance with:

  • CMS Conditions of Participation — F-Tag standards (see Recommendation section)
  • IDPH Title 77, Part 300 — Nursing and supportive care standards for LTC
  • Facility Medication Administration Policy & Procedure
  • Current CDC PrEP Clinical Practice Guidelines
  • All administration must be documented on the MAR in PCC at time of administration
  • Missed doses must be documented with reason code and communicated to the prescribing provider per facility protocol

CMS & IDPH Surveyor Standards — Medication Administration in LTC Facilities
🔍
The following tags and regulations are directly applicable to surveyors reviewing medication administration compliance and active orders in LTC. Facilities that cannot demonstrate timely, accurate administration of active PrEP orders are at risk for citation under these standards.
Tag / Citation Regulation / Code Requirement Summary PrEP Compliance Implication
CMS F755 42 CFR §483.45(a)–(b) The facility must provide pharmaceutical services (including administration) that meet the needs of each resident. A licensed pharmacist must review each resident's medication regimen at least monthly. Facility must ensure PrEP is dispensed, received, and administered without unnecessary delay. Gaps in administration will be reviewed by the facility pharmacist and may generate a flag for surveyors.
CMS F756 42 CFR §483.45(c) Psychotropic and other medications must be reviewed for appropriateness; dose reductions and non-pharmacological alternatives must be considered when clinically indicated. PrEP must be clinically justified and documented. Prescriber must ensure clinical indication is recorded in eCW and referenced in PCC.
CMS F758 42 CFR §483.45(e) Residents must not receive unnecessary medications. Each medication must have a documented indication and be monitored for effectiveness and adverse effects. Active PrEP orders must reflect ongoing HIV-negative status and documented risk indication. PCC orders must remain current and clinically supported.
CMS F760 42 CFR §483.45(g) Medications must be stored and labeled in accordance with applicable professional standards and state law. Medications requiring refrigeration must be stored appropriately. Xcel Rx-dispensed PrEP medications must be stored per facility protocol upon receipt. Injectable cabotegravir has specific storage requirements that must be followed.
CMS F761 42 CFR §483.45(h) Drugs and biologicals used in the facility must be labeled in accordance with currently accepted professional principles, including the appropriate accessory and cautionary instructions, and the expiration date, when applicable. Xcel Rx must provide properly labeled PrEP medications. Facility must not administer unlabeled or incorrectly labeled medications.
CMS F684 42 CFR §483.25 The facility must ensure that each resident receives care and services to attain or maintain the highest practicable physical, mental, and psychosocial well-being in accordance with the comprehensive assessment and plan of care. HIV prevention via PrEP is part of the resident's individualized care plan. Failure to administer PrEP as ordered directly compromises quality of care and is surveyor-actionable under F684.
CMS F697 42 CFR §483.25(k) Residents have the right to be free from unnecessary medications. Medications ordered must be administered as written. Nursing staff must document administration accurately on the MAR. Missed PrEP doses without documentation and clinical justification create discrepancies between active PCC orders and the MAR — a direct surveyor concern.
IDPH Title 77, Part 300, §300.1210 Illinois Admin. Code Title 77, Ch. I, Part 300 Illinois LTC facilities must maintain a complete and current medication administration record. All medications must be administered as ordered and documented contemporaneously with administration. Illinois state surveyors will verify MAR accuracy. PrEP orders in PCC must be active with the correct start date and every administration documented in real time.
IDPH Title 77, Part 300, §300.1230 Illinois Admin. Code Title 77 Facilities must have procedures for obtaining medications for residents, including emergency supply procedures, to avoid gaps in medication therapy. Facilities must have a process to track PrEP order status from Xcel Rx transmission through receipt and PCC entry, with escalation protocols when delays exceed acceptable windows.
IDPH Title 77, Part 300, §300.660 Illinois Admin. Code Title 77 Drug administration must be performed by licensed nursing personnel in accordance with the physician/prescriber's order and applicable law. Records must be maintained per state standards. Only licensed nursing staff may administer PrEP. Administration must be under an active, signed PCC order. Self-administration by residents requires a separate assessed capability and order.
✅
EAH Core Recommendation to Prevent Survey Deficiencies:
The single most important control is ensuring the PCC order start date = the date medication is physically received at the facility. This synchronization — enabled by the mandatory facility-to-prescriber notification step — eliminates the gap between an active order on the MAR and the absence of the medication, which is the primary driver of surveyor citations. All parties (prescriber, facility POC, nursing staff) must adhere to this communication-first workflow.
⚠️
Escalation Protocol: If the facility has not received the PrEP medication from Xcel Rx within 48 hours of prescription transmission, the facility POC must immediately contact the EAH Care Coordinator and the prescribing provider. The prescribing provider must contact Xcel Rx directly. If administration is delayed beyond 72 hours from the intended start, the prescribing provider must document the delay in eCW and PCC with a clinical notation and initiate a quality incident report per EAH policy.
